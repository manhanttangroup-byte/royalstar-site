Naples Homes for Sale: Prices, Storm Surge and Practical Advice

Updated October 6, 2026

Naples homes for sale run from condos in golf communities to historic cottages in Old Naples and Gulf-front estates well into the millions. It's one of Florida's most expensive markets, and its coast has taken storm surge from three hurricanes since 2022, so flood zone, elevation and insurance matter as much as the price. Rui Cunha's office is in Deerfield Beach, on the other side of the state, and he works with buyers across Florida, including Naples.

What it's like to live in Naples

Naples sits on the Gulf Coast in Collier County, in southwest Florida. The city itself is small: Florida's Office of Economic and Demographic Research estimated its population at 19,089 as of April 2025, while Collier County had 413,292 people (Florida EDR). Many homes with a Naples mailing address are in unincorporated Collier County, outside the city limits, which affects taxes and local rules. The area is known for its beaches, the Naples Pier, golf and its winter season.

Neighborhoods and communities

Redfin's 2024 neighborhood guide highlights these areas (Redfin):

  • Old Naples and Downtown. Historic charm near the beach, with shopping and dining on Third Street South and Fifth Avenue South.
  • Aqualane Shores, Port Royal and Royal Harbor. Waterfront neighborhoods on canals and Naples Bay, known for luxury homes and boating.
  • Coquina Sands, the Moorings and Park Shore. Neighborhoods north of downtown with beach access and a mix of homes and condos.

Outside the city, Naples also has many gated golf and country-club communities. Membership rules, fees and HOA costs vary a lot, so ask for them early.

Naples market snapshot

According to Momentum Research, computed from MLS records, the median closed price for Naples was about $600,000 in September 2026, up about 2.3% from a year earlier, with homes taking a median of 72 days to sell (Momentum).

That figure covers the wider Naples market. Inside the city's waterfront neighborhoods, prices are far higher, and a golf-community condo is a completely different market from a house on the Gulf. Ask Rui for a current comparison of recent sales for what you're actually considering.

Practical notes: storm surge, flood and insurance

Three surges in three years. The National Hurricane Center's reports show how exposed this coast is:

  • Hurricane Ian (2022) brought storm surge of 8 to 12 feet above ground to areas including North Naples; in Collier County, 33 buildings were destroyed and more than 3,500 had major damage (NHC, Ian report).
  • Hurricane Helene (September 2024) passed far offshore but still pushed 2 to 4 feet of surge into areas such as Naples and Marco Island (NHC, Helene report).
  • Hurricane Milton (October 2024) made landfall well to the north, at Siesta Key, yet surge of 3 to 5 feet reached the coast from Bonita Beach through Marco Island, and the Naples Bay tide gauge measured 5.08 feet above the normal high-tide line (NHC, Milton report).

What it means for you. Before you make an offer:

  • Check the FEMA flood zone and ask for an elevation certificate.
  • Ask whether the home flooded in Ian, Helene or Milton, what was repaired and whether the work was permitted. Under FEMA rules, a home in a high-risk flood zone with damage above 50% of its value must be brought up to current flood rules, which can mean elevating or rebuilding it (St. Pete Catalyst).
  • Get real homeowners and flood insurance quotes. Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation puts the statewide average homeowners premium at about $3,736 a year, including wind, in the admitted market (Florida Realtors, September 2026). A waterfront home can cost much more.
  • If you'll insure through Citizens, flood coverage is becoming mandatory as that rule phases in through 2027 (News4JAX).

Condos. Many Naples buyers choose condos. Florida law requires milestone inspections for buildings of three or more stories (at 30 years, or 25 if the local building department requires it, then every 10 years — SB 154, 2023) and a Structural Integrity Reserve Study with funded reserves (Rimkus). Ask for the reserve study, budget, inspection reports, storm-repair history and rental rules, since associations can limit short-term rentals.

Homestead. If the home will be your primary residence, ask a CPA about Florida's homestead exemption. Second homes and non-residents don't qualify.

Schools

Naples is served by Collier County Public Schools. Use the district's tools to confirm assigned schools for any address, and check school grades with the Florida Department of Education.

Getting around

Naples is car-dependent. I-75 connects it north to Fort Myers and east across the Everglades (Alligator Alley) to Broward County. Seasonal traffic in winter is much heavier, so test your routes in high season.

FAQ

What is the median home price in Naples, FL?

According to Momentum Research, the median closed price for Naples was about $600,000 in September 2026, up about 2.3% from a year earlier. Waterfront neighborhoods inside the city cost far more, so compare recent sales for the area you want.

Did Naples flood in recent hurricanes?

Yes. The National Hurricane Center reported 8 to 12 feet of storm surge in North Naples during Hurricane Ian in 2022, and both Helene and Milton pushed surge into the Naples area in 2024. Always check the flood zone, elevation and the home's storm history.

Can I rent out a Naples condo short-term?

It depends on the association. Condo associations and HOAs can set minimum rental periods or other limits, so read the documents before you buy if rental income matters to you.
