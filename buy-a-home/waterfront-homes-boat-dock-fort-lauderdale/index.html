Waterfront Homes With a Boat Dock in Fort Lauderdale: What to Check Before You Offer

Updated October 14, 2026

A waterfront home with a dock in Fort Lauderdale is only as useful as the route from your dock to open water. Before the price, check three things: the lowest bridge between the house and the ocean (against your boat's height), the depth of the canal at low tide (against your boat's draft), and the condition of the seawall, which can cost more than the dock. Add the flood zone and insurance quote and you have the real cost of owning it. Rui Cunha is a broker at Royal Star Corp in Coral Springs and helps buyers across Broward, including Fort Lauderdale. This page is a checklist, not a ranking of streets.

Ocean access, no fixed bridges, fixed bridges

Fort Lauderdale's canals connect to the Intracoastal Waterway and from there to the ocean through Port Everglades. The route is what matters:

  • Direct ocean access / no fixed bridges. No fixed bridge between the dock and the inlet, so height limits come only from drawbridges, which must open to let tall boats through. Drawbridges follow opening schedules set by federal rules, so a tall boat may have to wait (33 CFR Part 117).
  • Fixed bridges on the route. A fixed bridge has a set clearance, and your boat has to fit under it at high tide. Fixed bridges over the Atlantic Intracoastal are supposed to clear 65 feet, but that is a guideline, not a guarantee, and tides and storms change it (Soundings). Residential canal bridges are usually much lower, so confirm every bridge on the route.

Measure your boat from the waterline to its highest fixed point, including radar and antennas, and compare with the posted clearance of every bridge on the way out. Take a test run before you buy if you can. Sailboats and tall boats are the most affected.

Canal depth and tides

Depth varies by canal and drops at low tide. Use NOAA's nautical chart and tide predictions to compare the canal's charted depth with your boat's draft, and ask the seller where the channel and the dock are shallow. Also check what high tides do to the street and the yard: the city has a page on king tides and high tides.

Seawalls: the biggest line item

A seawall holds the soil under your yard. In March 2023 the city commission passed a tidal barrier ordinance that raised the minimum seawall height from 3.9 to 5 feet NAVD, to match a Broward County standard (City of Fort Lauderdale presentation). According to the city, an owner must raise a seawall when building a new one, when the existing wall is in significant disrepair (more than 50% damaged), or when the owner is cited because tidal water is breaching the wall and affecting a neighbor or the right of way. Owners must also keep the wall in good repair, and the city allows 365 days to fix a cited problem. Docks attached to a new or rebuilt barrier may sit up to 12 inches above the maximum seawall height.

A buyer inherits this. Before you offer:

  • Ask for the seawall's age, type and repair history. Concrete, vinyl, steel and coral rock walls age differently.
  • Budget for replacement. One Fort Lauderdale contractor estimates full replacement at about $1,500 to $2,500 per linear foot, or $120,000 to $250,000 for an 80 to 100 foot lot, with engineering and permits often $5,000 to $15,000 extra (Josh Doti Group, contractor estimate, 2026). Prices vary a lot by site, so get quotes from licensed marine contractors.
  • Check the habitat rule. The city's draft FAQ says a new seawall where none existed must add habitat enhancement. Ask the contractor.

Dock and boat-lift permits

Docks, lifts and seawall work generally need Broward County environmental approval and a city building permit, and in some cases state or federal review. Broward's April 2025 bulletin says a "General License" covers a structure under 500 square feet or an exact replacement of an existing dock without riprap, and it is usually processed in 7 to 10 days; larger projects need a full license (Broward County). Ask the seller for permits for the dock, lift and seawall, because unpermitted work can cause trouble at closing.

Flood zone and flood insurance

Check the property's flood zone on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center and get flood and wind quotes before you go under contract. Under FEMA's Risk Rating 2.0, an elevation certificate is no longer required to buy a federal flood policy, but if you provide one, FEMA uses whichever first-floor height is more favorable to you, so it can lower the premium (FloodSmart). Your lender may still ask for one. Citizens policyholders must carry flood coverage as that rule phases in through 2027 (News4JAX). Seawalls are often excluded from homeowners policies (Waypoint Inspection), so ask your insurance agent exactly what is covered.

Inspections specific to the water

A standard home inspection usually does not cover the seawall, dock and lift. Hire a marine engineer or seawall specialist, and ask whether the inspection includes the part below the waterline, because some inspectors work from ground level only (Waypoint Inspection). A Fort Lauderdale source suggests a professional seawall inspection every 3 to 5 years and after major storms (Josh Doti Group). Also look for soil loss or sinkholes behind the wall, cracks, leaning panels and rusted tie rods. FAQ

What does "no fixed bridges" mean for a Fort Lauderdale canal home?

It means no fixed bridge between the dock and the ocean, so only drawbridges limit height, and they open on set schedules. Confirm the route yourself, because clearance varies by lot and tide.

Who pays for a seawall repair when I buy a waterfront home?

The owner. In Fort Lauderdale the owner must keep the seawall in good repair and, if cited, has 365 days to fix it. Get a professional inspection and a repair estimate before you offer.

Do I need a permit to replace a dock or add a boat lift?

Usually yes: Broward County environmental approval plus a city building permit, and sometimes state or federal review. Ask the seller for existing permits.

Do I need an elevation certificate to insure a waterfront home?

Not for a FEMA flood policy under Risk Rating 2.0, but it can lower the premium, and your lender may ask for it. Get quotes before you go under contract.

Can I buy a waterfront home in Fort Lauderdale with an ITIN loan?

Many buyers use ITIN mortgages, subject to lender approval. See the ITIN mortgage guide.
