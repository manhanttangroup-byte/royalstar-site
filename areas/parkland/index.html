Parkland Homes for Sale: Prices, Communities and Schools

Updated October 6, 2026

Parkland FL homes for sale are mostly single-family houses in planned, often gated communities, and they sit at the top of the north Broward price range. Recent closings put the median near $1.2 million, with most sales between about $910,000 and $1.5 million. Families usually come for space, newer homes and the public schools. Rui Cunha, Broker at Royal Star Corp, works Parkland from his office in nearby Deerfield Beach.

What it's like to live in Parkland

Parkland is a residential city in northern Broward County. Its northern edge is the Palm Beach County line, with Coconut Creek to the east, Coral Springs to the south and west, and the Everglades beyond its western edge (Wikipedia). Florida's Office of Economic and Demographic Research estimated the population at 38,427 as of April 1, 2025 (Florida EDR).

It's an inland city with no beach, so buyers trade a longer drive to the coast for bigger lots and quieter streets. Almost every sale is a house: about 95% of recent closings were single-family homes (Resideline).

Communities

Parkland is made up largely of planned communities, each with its own HOA, amenities and rules. Market trackers follow communities such as these (Move With Momentum):

  • Parkland Bay
  • Heron Bay
  • Miralago at Parkland
  • Parkland Royale

Prices, HOA fees and rules differ a lot from one community to the next, even when they're side by side. Ask Rui for recent sales inside the specific communities you're considering.

Parkland market snapshot

Resideline tracked 356 closings in Parkland over the six months to October 3, 2026. The median closing price was $1,175,000, and the middle half of sales closed between $910,000 and $1,525,000. Homes took a median of 31 days to go under contract (Resideline).

For a second view, the Broward County Property Appraiser's 2026 tax roll puts the median market value of Parkland's 11,366 single-family homes at $990,370. Its 436 condo units have a median of $457,270 (BCPA, single-family; BCPA, condos). Those are appraiser estimates, not sale prices, but they show how far above the Broward-wide single-family median of $514,200 Parkland sits.

Prices vary widely by community, lot and age of the home, so ask Rui for a current comparison before you make an offer.

Practical notes: HOAs, condos, insurance and flood

HOAs. Most Parkland homes are in HOA communities. Before you sign, read the HOA budget, rules (rentals, pets, vehicles, exterior changes) and any planned assessments.

Condos. Parkland has few condos, but the state rules still apply to buildings of three or more stories: a milestone inspection at 30 years, or 25 if the local building department requires it (SB 154, 2023), plus a reserve study and funded reserves (Rimkus).

Insurance and flood. Parkland is inland. FEMA's newer pricing raised flood premiums most in coastal Broward and Palm Beach, while many inland areas stayed roughly stable (WLRN). Flood zones are still set address by address, so check the FEMA map and get insurance quotes before you sign. Citizens policyholders must carry flood coverage as the requirement phases in through 2027 (News4JAX).

Property taxes. Higher home values mean higher tax bills. A primary residence may qualify for Florida's homestead exemption. Ask a CPA how it applies to you.

Schools

Parkland is served by Broward County Public Schools. Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School serves almost all of the city, and Westglades Middle School serves most of it, with small sections zoned to Coral Springs schools. Elementary schools in the city include Riverglades, Park Trails and Heron Heights (Wikipedia). Boundaries can change, so confirm the zoned schools for any address with the district and check grades on the Florida Department of Education site.

Getting around

Parkland sits between Coral Springs and Coconut Creek, with Boca Raton just across the county line. Most residents drive. The beach and I-95 are farther away than from coastal cities like Deerfield Beach, so test your commute at rush hour before you commit.

FAQ

How much does a home in Parkland FL cost?

Resideline reported a median closing price of $1,175,000 for the six months to October 3, 2026, with the middle half of sales between $910,000 and $1,525,000. Prices vary a lot by community and lot.

Which schools serve Parkland?

Broward County Public Schools. Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School and Westglades Middle School serve almost all of the city, with a few sections zoned to Coral Springs schools. Always confirm the zoned schools for the exact address.

Is Parkland in a flood zone?

Parkland is inland, and FEMA's newer pricing hit coastal areas hardest. Flood zones are still mapped address by address, so check the FEMA map and get an insurance quote for the specific home.
